7 Retirement & Savings Mistakes Many People Don't Realize They're Making

A Simple Educational Guide to Help You Better Understand Important Retirement & Savings Decisions

Loretta Stewart
Life & Retirement Advisor
Prosperity Life & Financial Solutions
414-441-1177 | www.prosperitylfs.com
Retirement and savings decisions can feel overwhelming, especially when you're trying to protect the money you worked hard to build. Many people make critical mistakes simply because they didn't know better—or didn't have the right information at the right time.
This guide highlights 7 common retirement and savings mistakes that many people don't realize they're making. Whether you have $25,000, $100,000, or more in savings, 401(k)s, IRAs, or other retirement accounts, understanding these mistakes can help you make better decisions and protect your nest egg for the future.
1

Keeping Too Much Money in Low-Interest Savings Accounts

Many people keep large portions of their retirement savings in regular savings accounts or money market accounts earning minimal interest. While these accounts offer safety, they often fail to keep pace with inflation, meaning your purchasing power quietly erodes over time.

Instead, explore options like CDs, high-yield savings alternatives, and guaranteed income products that provide better returns while still protecting your principal. The goal is growth without unnecessary risk.

2

Forgetting About Old Retirement Accounts

Over the course of your career, you may have opened numerous 401(k)s, 403(b)s, TSPs, or IRAs with different employers. It's easy to lose track of these accounts, and forgotten accounts can create problems later.

Consolidating old accounts can simplify your financial life, reduce fees, and make it easier to manage your overall strategy. Plus, you won't miss important distribution deadlines or tax implications. Take time to locate all your accounts and evaluate whether consolidation makes sense.

3

Not Understanding All Available Retirement Options

Many retirement account holders don't fully understand the options available to them. Whether it's the difference between a traditional IRA and a Roth IRA, or how annuities work, gaps in knowledge can lead to missed opportunities.

Taking time to understand your options—such as employer plans, IRAs, annuities, safe money alternatives, and life insurance products—helps you make decisions that align with your unique situation. There's no one-size-fits-all answer, but knowing your choices is essential.

4

Waiting Too Long to Create an Income Plan

Having savings is important, but many people don't think strategically about how to convert those savings into reliable retirement income. Without a clear plan, retirees often withdraw money haphazardly, which can deplete accounts faster than expected and increase tax burdens.

Creating a retirement income strategy years before you retire gives you time to optimize decisions and adjust as needed. A well-thought-out plan can help ensure your money lasts as long as you do.

5

Overlooking Tax Considerations

Many people don't realize how much taxes can impact their retirement. Different account types have different tax treatment, and withdrawal strategies can significantly affect your overall tax bill in retirement.

Understanding concepts like Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs), tax-efficient withdrawal sequencing, and Roth conversions can help you keep more of your hard-earned money. Small tax-smart moves today can result in meaningful savings over time.

6

Not Reviewing Beneficiaries Regularly

Many people set up their beneficiaries years ago and forget about them. Life changes—marriages, divorces, births, deaths—but beneficiary designations often sit unchanged, which can result in your assets going to unintended recipients.

Review your beneficiary designations on all retirement accounts, life insurance policies, and other assets at least every few years or whenever your life situation changes. This ensures your legacy reflects your current intentions and values.

7

Waiting Too Long to Ask Questions

Many people struggle with decisions about their retirement but put off seeking professional guidance. They worry about costs, feel uncertain about what to ask, or simply don't know where to start.

The earlier you get answers to your questions, the more time you have to act on that knowledge. Whether it's understanding your options, evaluating your current strategy, or creating a comprehensive retirement plan, getting clarity sooner rather than later puts you in a stronger position.
